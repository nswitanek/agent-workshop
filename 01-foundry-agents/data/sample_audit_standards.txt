Sample Audit Standards Reference

This document serves as a condensed knowledge base for the workshop agent examples.

Materiality

Materiality determination is a critical step in audit planning (AU-C Section 320, ISA 320).

- Overall materiality is typically calculated as a percentage of a chosen benchmark (e.g., 5% of pre-tax income, 0.5–1% of total revenue, or 1–2% of total assets).
- Performance materiality is set lower than overall materiality (typically 50–75%) to reduce the risk that uncorrected and undetected misstatements exceed overall materiality.
- Clearly trivial threshold is typically set at 3–5% of overall materiality — misstatements below this amount need not be accumulated.
- Materiality should be reassessed as the audit progresses if new information changes the auditor's judgment.

Risk Assessment

Risk of material misstatement must be assessed at both the financial statement level and the assertion level (AU-C 315, AS 2110).

Inherent Risk Factors
- Nature of the entity and its industry
- Complexity of transactions
- Degree of estimation and judgment
- Susceptibility to fraud
- Recent changes in operations or accounting standards

Control Risk Factors
- Design effectiveness of internal controls
- Operating effectiveness (tested through walkthroughs and sampling)
- IT general controls and application controls
- Segregation of duties

Fraud Considerations

Per AU-C 240 / AS 2401, auditors must:
- Maintain professional skepticism throughout the audit
- Discuss fraud risks with the engagement team (brainstorming session)
- Identify and assess risks of material misstatement due to fraud
- Respond to assessed fraud risks with appropriate audit procedures
- Revenue recognition is presumed to be a fraud risk unless rebutted

Internal Controls

Understanding internal controls is required per AU-C 315 / AS 2201:

1. Control environment — tone at the top, governance, ethics
2. Risk assessment process — how management identifies risks
3. Information systems — relevant to financial reporting
4. Control activities — policies and procedures for control objectives
5. Monitoring — ongoing and separate evaluations of control effectiveness

Sampling

Audit sampling (AU-C 530) approaches:
- Statistical sampling — allows quantification of sampling risk
- Non-statistical sampling — relies on auditor judgment
- Sample size factors: expected deviation rate, tolerable deviation rate, risk of overreliance
- Monetary unit sampling (MUS) is commonly used for substantive testing of account balances
